# Chapter 5: Discussion and Conclusion

---

## 5.1 Introduction

This chapter interprets the empirical findings reported in Chapter 4, situates them within the recent traffic and ridership forecasting literature, and draws out their theoretical and practical implications for Malaysian public transit planning. The discussion is organised around the five thematic conclusions established in Section 4.10: the superiority of the proposed hybrid HMT-TSF architecture, the trade-off between accuracy and generalisation in recurrent baselines, the optimality of the 14-day look-back window, robustness under the MCO structural break, and the interpretability of the learned feature hierarchy. The chapter then presents the practical implications of deploying the system in the Malaysian context, acknowledges the limitations of the study, proposes directions for future work, and closes with the overall conclusion.

---

## 5.2 Discussion of Key Findings

### 5.2.1 Hybrid Multi-Modal Architectures Outperform Single-Paradigm Models

The central finding of this study is that a hybrid architecture combining multi-scale temporal convolution, feature-graph message passing, and regime-aware normalisation outperforms every single-paradigm baseline for seven-day Malaysian transit ridership forecasting. The evidence is unambiguous: HMT-TSF achieved a Combined% of 81.82 and R² of 0.802 at the headline `nomco_lb14` configuration, leading the best baseline (Informer, 79.13%) by 2.69 Combined% and reducing daily ridership MAE by 17.2% (Section 4.7.2); even the best tuned baseline (Informer tuned, 79.99%) remained 1.83 Combined% behind. This margin is consistent with the broader pattern in the recent literature, in which architectures that fuse complementary inductive biases outperform their components in isolation: Chen et al. (2022) demonstrated that coupling CNN local pattern extraction with bidirectional recurrence reduces traffic prediction error beyond either mechanism alone, and Wu et al. (2025) showed that combining learned graph adjacency with multi-scale dilated temporal convolution yields more stable traffic flow predictions than fixed-structure alternatives. The analysis in Section 4.2.1 sharpens this interpretation: the top eight baselines were compressed within a 1.5 Combined% band, indicating a saturated accuracy regime in which no single architectural mechanism — bidirectionality, pattern attention, graph convolution, or sparse attention — could break away from the field. HMT-TSF escaped this saturation not by deepening any one mechanism but by operating three in parallel and fusing them through a learned softmax gate, suggesting that the marginal returns to architectural innovation in transit forecasting now lie in principled fusion rather than refinement of individual paradigms. This finding directly establishes the value of the proposed model and frames the generalisation analysis that follows.

### 5.2.2 Test-Set Accuracy Conceals Systematic Overfitting in Recurrent Baselines

A second major finding is that nominal test-set accuracy is an unreliable indicator of model quality, because the strongest recurrent baselines achieve their rankings partly through memorisation. The fit diagnostics in Section 4.6 showed that all six LSTM-family models and both simple-convolution graph models recorded zero good-fit verdicts across all twelve evaluated configurations, with train/validation gap ratios spanning 3.3× to 13.6× for the LSTM family and reaching 38.61× for PDR-STGCN — even though BiLSTM and TPA-LSTM ranked second and third on test accuracy with R² of 0.776. This dissociation between accuracy and fit quality echoes Topilin et al. (2025), who observed that sequential CNN-LSTM architectures memorise characteristic local temporal patterns at the expense of adaptability, and contrasts with the implicit regularisation that Song et al. (2024) attribute to ProbSparse attention, which restricts active queries to a logarithmic subset and thereby suppresses the fitting of high-frequency noise — a mechanism reflected in Informer's unique 6/6 clean-fit record at base configuration. The analytical implication is methodological as much as architectural: had this study reported only test-set Combined%, BiLSTM would appear nearly equivalent to Informer, yet the diagnostics reveal that the two models occupy opposite ends of the generalisation spectrum. The uniformly negative effect of hyperparameter tuning on fit quality (good-fit rate falling from 26% to 14%, Section 4.6) reinforces the same caution: added capacity purchased small and inconsistent test-set gains at a consistent generalisation cost. For deployment decisions, therefore, fit diagnostics must be evaluated alongside accuracy — a practice this study operationalised and which directly motivates the robustness analysis in the next section.

### 5.2.3 The 14-Day Look-Back Window Is Decisively Optimal Under Normal Conditions

The look-back sensitivity sweep yields a clear and practically valuable design rule: for a seven-day forecast horizon under normal operating conditions, a 14-day historical window is optimal, and longer context actively harms most architectures. Across the sweep in Section 4.4, lb14 was optimal for ten of sixteen baseline variants, lb28 for six, and lb56 for none; HMT-TSF's own profile was single-peaked at lb14 (81.82%), declining monotonically to 74.17% at lb84 (Section 4.7.1). The most severe case was STFGNN, which collapsed from 73.94% at lb14 to 43.25% at lb56 with R² of −0.112 — worse than naive mean prediction — because the longer temporal profiles used to construct its temporal adjacency matrix become noisy enough to supply misleading structural priors (Chang et al., 2025). Conversely, MTGNN's near-perfect window stability (77.93/78.11/77.76) confirms Wu et al.'s (2025) account of multi-scale dilated inception preventing any single temporal scale from dominating. The analysis suggests that ridership predictability is concentrated in the two most recent weekly cycles: fourteen days span exactly two full periods of the dominant weekly seasonality identified by the SHAP analysis (`day_of_week` being the only temporal feature in the top ten, Section 4.8.1), so additional history contributes redundancy and noise rather than signal. This is consistent with Ma and Zhang's (2025) finding that decomposition-based forecasters perform stably only under sequence lengths appropriate to the dominant periodicity of the signal. The single exception — MCO-regime operation, where HMT-TSF preferred lb56 because longer context lets the model average through the lockdown discontinuity — transitions naturally to the question of structural-break robustness.

### 5.2.4 Structural Inductive Biases Determine Robustness Under Distributional Shift

The MCO analysis demonstrates that robustness to structural breaks is governed less by nominal accuracy than by the rigidity or adaptivity of a model's structural assumptions. The evidence from Section 4.5 spans a dramatic degradation range: HMT-TSF lost only 5.71 Combined% when the pandemic period was included, while STFGNN and CNN-LSTM sequential lost over 32 points each and fell below naive mean-prediction (R² of −0.306 and −0.050 respectively) — despite CNN-LSTM ranking eighth in the default configuration. Models with strong structural constraints proved disproportionately robust: STGCN's fixed Chebyshev graph acted as a regulariser limiting prediction drift (Deng, 2025), and Informer's sparse attention selectively engaged the most predictive query-key pairs across the regime boundary (Song et al., 2024). The analysis reveals a consistent expressiveness-stability trade-off: the most flexible learned representations (STFGNN's dual graphs, CNN-LSTM's single-stream CNN encoding) memorised pre-break regularities that became liabilities after the break, while constrained or explicitly regime-aware designs transferred. HMT-TSF resolved this trade-off rather than merely choosing a side — its Reversible Instance Normalisation removed within-window amplitude drift while the Regime Gating Embedding adjusted prediction priors per operational period, allowing it to retain both the highest absolute MCO accuracy (76.11%) and a perfect good-fit record under the break (Section 4.7.5). For a country whose ridership history contains an 85–95% demand collapse, this finding is not academic: any production forecasting system for Malaysian transit must be evaluated against, and architecturally prepared for, regime discontinuity. The interpretability of what the models actually learned is taken up next.

### 5.2.5 The Learned Feature Hierarchy Is Domain-Consistent and Enables Principled Feature Reduction

The final discussion point is that the model's learned feature importance is interpretable, theoretically coherent, and actionable. SHAP analysis (Section 4.8, corrected labels) ranked historical ridership autocorrelation first — `total_ridership` appeared in the top-15 of all ten configurations — followed by calendar structure (`is_weekend`, fourth; `days_since_last_public_hol` and `day_of_week` also in the top ten), with diesel price level (seventh) and Penang rainfall (eighth) as the leading external covariates. These rankings quantify three established demand mechanisms: the weekly commuting cycle and holiday return dynamics as the dominant systematic drivers, a secondary mode-substitution effect through fuel prices, and weather-induced accessibility effects on station access, consistent with the multi-source demand drivers documented for urban rail systems by Cui et al. (2025). The practical consequence was the HMT-TSF-FR variant: removing the 26 features with zero or near-zero SHAP contribution — the entire 17-feature static group plus nine near-constant administered fuel-price columns — cost only 0.05 Combined% at the headline configuration while shrinking the graph adjacency from 79×79 to 53×53 and reducing explainability memory requirements by roughly a third (Section 4.9). The analysis here carries a substantive insight about static spatial descriptors: population, GTFS capacity, and POI features were redundant not because spatial structure is irrelevant, but because time-invariant structural capacity is already implicitly encoded in historical ridership levels given sufficient look-back context. The one caveat — HMT-TSF-FR's sharp degradation at `mco_lb84`, where near-zero features evidently helped navigate the lockdown-spanning window — reinforces the chapter's recurring theme that feature and architecture decisions validated under normal conditions must be re-validated under regime shift. Together, these five findings ground the implications discussed below.

## 5.3 Implications of the Study

### 5.3.1 Theoretical Implications

This study contributes three generalisable insights to the spatio-temporal forecasting literature. First, it provides systematic evidence — across sixteen baseline variants, three look-back windows, two distributional regimes, and tuned/untuned conditions — that the features-as-nodes graph formulation transfers graph-based traffic forecasting methods (Chang et al., 2025; Deng, 2025; Wu et al., 2025) from sensor-network settings to multivariate national ridership data, while exposing where that transfer breaks down (temporal-adjacency construction at long windows). Second, it demonstrates that fit diagnostics (gap ratio, validation drift) constitute a necessary complement to test-set accuracy in model comparison studies, materially reordering the practical ranking of models relative to accuracy-only evaluation. Third, it establishes regime gating combined with reversible instance normalisation as an effective and lightweight mechanism for structural-break robustness, extending the regime-shift observations of Song et al. (2024) from attention sparsity into an explicit, learnable regime representation.

### 5.3.2 Practical Implications for Malaysian Transit

As a proof of concept, the system is most immediately applicable to developing transit markets — states such as Johor and the East Malaysian corridors — where new services can be planned around demand forecasts from the outset rather than retrofitted onto legacy operations. For established, continuously operating networks such as the Klang Valley and Penang systems, the findings support phased integration: the model can run in parallel with existing planning processes without disrupting current services, with forecast-informed decisions adopted incrementally as confidence accumulates through walk-forward validation of the kind reported in Section 4.7.4.

From a business perspective, accurate seven-day demand forecasts enable operational strategy — frequency setting, rolling-stock allocation, and staffing — to be matched to predicted demand. The 17.2% MAE reduction over the best baseline translates directly into tighter capacity planning margins: operators can scale capacity by procuring additional cabins for existing lines rather than committing capital to entirely new rail infrastructure, deferring expansion until forecast demand justifies it. From a sustainability perspective, demand-matched service levels reduce empty-running and the associated energy cost, and the SHAP-detected (secondary) fuel-price signal gives policymakers an initial quantified link between fuel subsidy policy and transit uptake. From a scalability perspective, the features-as-nodes design is inherently general: a new service line enters the model as additional feature nodes with a recomputed correlation adjacency, requiring no geographic re-engineering, and the HMT-TSF-FR result shows the feature set can be kept lean as the network grows.

## 5.4 Limitations of the Study

Seven limitations qualify the findings. First, the study forecasts at national daily granularity with service lines as features; it does not produce station-level or intra-day forecasts, which limits direct applicability to platform-level crowd management of the kind addressed by station-level studies (Wei et al., 2023; Cui et al., 2025). Second, the evaluation relies on a single national dataset; although walk-forward validation across three temporal blocks (Section 4.7.4) confirms temporal stability, cross-network external validity — for example, to bus-dominant or polycentric systems — remains untested. Third, the dataset is batch-historical: kiosk and vehicle ridership records are ingested after the fact, and the data collection process itself introduces integrity risks (missing days, delayed reporting) that the pipeline imputes but cannot eliminate at source. Fourth, the MCO period provides only one observed structural break; the Regime Gating Embedding's K=3 regimes are calibrated to that specific discontinuity, and its behaviour under a future, differently shaped shock is necessarily extrapolative.

Fifth, all reported results are single-run point estimates obtained with a fixed random seed; no repeated-seed variance, bootstrap confidence intervals, or forecast-comparison significance tests (e.g., Diebold–Mariano) were computed, so the statistical separability of closely ranked models is unestablished — sub-percentage-point ranking differences should be treated as indicative. Sixth, the study addresses transit unreliability from the demand side only: it forecasts ridership to enable proactive capacity and headway decisions, but does not directly model supply-side reliability events (delays, breakdowns, cancellations), for which no public Malaysian dataset exists at daily granularity. Seventh, the autoregressive lag features in the MCO-excluded dataset bridge the excluded window: at the start of the post-MCO period, `ridership_lag_{7,14,28}` reference dates inside the removed 2020-03-18 – 2021-12-31 interval, so the "MCO-excluded" condition retains indirect MCO information through its lag channel — a deliberate design choice (it avoids fabricating lag values) that should nonetheless be borne in mind when interpreting the no-MCO results.

## 5.5 Future Works

Seven directions follow directly from the findings and limitations.

**Statistical validation of model rankings.** The most immediate methodological extension is replication: re-running each headline configuration across multiple random seeds to attach variance estimates to every reported metric, complemented by Diebold–Mariano tests (or bootstrap confidence intervals over test-window errors) for pairwise forecast comparisons. This converts the current indicative rankings into statistically defensible claims and is a prerequisite for journal publication of the comparative results.

**Supply-side reliability data integration.** To close the loop between demand forecasting and unreliability mitigation, future work should incorporate supply-side reliability signals — automatic vehicle location (AVL) traces, on-time-performance records, disruption and maintenance logs — as both additional covariates and as forecast targets in their own right (e.g., delay-risk prediction). This is the direct path from the present demand-side analytics layer to a system that predicts and pre-empts unreliability events themselves.

**Real-time data integration.** The highest-value extension is ingesting real-time feeds — service alerts, trip updates, and vehicle positions — to complement the daily batch pipeline. This would shift the system from offline planning support toward operational decision support, including passenger-facing waiting-time estimation. The Informer-style sparse attention already embedded in the architecture is computationally suited to the longer, finer-grained sequences that real-time data implies (Song et al., 2024).

**Data integrity at the source.** Improving collection from kiosks and vehicles — validation at ingestion, automated anomaly flagging, and reconciliation across fare-collection channels — would reduce the imputation burden and strengthen the autocorrelation signals that SHAP identified as the dominant predictive drivers. Multi-source fusion approaches of the kind described by Cui et al. (2025) offer a template for reconciling heterogeneous collection channels.

**Spatial and temporal disaggregation.** Extending the features-as-nodes formulation to station-level nodes with genuine geographic adjacency would connect this work to the station-level passenger flow literature (Wei et al., 2023) and enable intra-day forecasting, at the cost of a substantially larger graph; MTGNN-style learned adjacency (Wu et al., 2025) and the demonstrated SHAP-based feature reduction provide a path to keeping that growth tractable.

**Generalisation to new service lines and networks.** The scalability claim in Section 5.3.2 should be validated empirically by introducing a genuinely new line (for example, a future Johor transit service) and measuring cold-start forecast quality as its history accumulates, including transfer-learning warm starts from established Klang Valley lines.

**Regime modelling beyond the MCO.** Future work should generalise the regime mechanism from a fixed K=3 embedding to an open-set regime detector capable of recognising novel structural breaks online — fuel subsidy reform, fare restructuring, or new-line openings — so that the robustness demonstrated retrospectively in Section 4.5 becomes a prospective operational property.

---

## 5.6 Conclusion

This study set out to determine whether a purpose-built hybrid architecture could outperform established deep-learning paradigms for seven-day Malaysian transit ridership forecasting, and to characterise the conditions under which each paradigm succeeds or fails. The answer is affirmative and well-bounded. HMT-TSF leads all sixteen baseline variants on every headline metric (Combined% 81.82, R² 0.802, MAE 57,553), is the most robust model under the MCO structural break (Δ −5.71), and is the only model family with a perfect good-fit record across all evaluated configurations. The comparative evaluation additionally yields durable design guidance independent of the proposed model: a 14-day look-back window is decisively optimal for a seven-day horizon under normal conditions; test-set accuracy must be read jointly with fit diagnostics, since the strongest recurrent baselines are chronic overfitters; and structural inductive biases, not nominal accuracy, determine survival under distributional shift. The SHAP analysis confirms that the model's predictive reasoning — ridership autocorrelation, weekly and holiday calendar structure, and secondary fuel-price and weather signals — is consistent with transit demand theory, and the 53-feature reduced variant shows the system can be made leaner without sacrificing accuracy. Taken together, these results establish a credible, interpretable, and deployment-ready foundation for demand-responsive transit planning in Malaysia: immediately as a proof of concept for emerging networks in Johor and East Malaysia, and through phased integration for the established Klang Valley and Penang systems, with real-time data integration as the clearest path from planning tool to operational system.
